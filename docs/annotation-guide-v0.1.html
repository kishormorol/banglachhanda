Chhanda Annotation Guide

How to mark syllable structure, foot boundaries and meter in Bangla verse, so that two annotators working separately produce the same thing.

Drafted 16 Sep 2026 Status: unreviewed by a prosody specialist Target: 300-line pilot

Read this before anything else

This draft was written from general knowledge of Bangla prosody, not from a pinned authority with the book open. The layer design and the annotation format are sound and are the parts worth arguing about. The specific rules are provisional. Every rule marked যাচাই must be checked against the reference the project pins before an annotator sees this document — authorities differ on exactly the edge cases that decide inter-annotator agreement.

Nothing here should be presented to annotators as settled until a prosody specialist has signed off on §2 and §5.

§1

What this annotation is for

The corpus supports three things, in order: a scansion model that labels syllable weight and foot boundaries; a rule-based baseline that derives the same labels from the textbook rules; and an automatic metric that scores generated verse for form adherence.

That ordering has a consequence for the guideline. The annotation must record what is observable in the text, and leave what is derivable by rule to the rule engine. If annotators hand-label matra counts, the corpus cannot be used to test whether the textbook rules actually predict matra counts — the answer gets baked into the data. So annotators label structure; matra is computed.

Design question for the collaborator Is there any meter where the matra value cannot be derived from syllable structure plus position, and genuinely requires the annotator's ear? If so, that meter needs a hand-labelled matra layer and must be kept separate from the derived ones.
§2

Pin one authority

Bangla prosody has several standard treatments, and they do not agree in the margins. The project must pin exactly one as normative, cite its edition, and record every departure from it.

  • Prabodhchandra Sen — the classification of অক্ষরবৃত্ত / মাত্রাবৃত্ত / স্বরবৃত্ত in general use descends from his work; the natural default.
  • Amulyadhan Mukhopadhyay — closer analysis of Tagore's practice, useful where Sen's categories strain.
  • Jibendra Singha Ray — widely used as a teaching text, so likely what annotators recruited from a Bangla department already know.

Recruiting annotators trained on one authority and grading them against another is a known way to manufacture disagreement that looks like model-relevant signal but is bookkeeping.

Decision needed Which authority, which edition, and does the naming follow it exactly — including whether পয়ার is treated as a form of অক্ষরবৃত্ত or as its own category in the label set?
§3

The layers

Six layers. The first four are what an annotator produces; the last two are derived and shown to the annotator only for checking.

L0

Text and register

The line as printed, plus a register tag: sadhu or chalit. Not cosmetic — করিয়া and করে differ in syllable count, so register changes the scansion of the same sense.

L1

Syllabification

Split the line into syllables (অক্ষর). This is the layer that carries most of the disagreement, and most of the value.

L2

Syllable type

Each syllable is open (মুক্তাক্ষর) or closed (বদ্ধাক্ষর) — whether it ends in a consonant. Meter-independent, which is exactly why it is annotated rather than derived.

L3

Word and foot boundaries

Word boundaries come from the orthography. Foot (পর্ব) boundaries are the annotator's judgement about where the line divides, plus a flag for a short final foot (অতিপর্ব).

L4

Meter class — derived, then confirmed

The rule engine proposes a class from L1–L3; the annotator confirms or overrides with a reason. An override is a finding, not an error: it is where the rules fail.

L5

Matra — derived only

Computed from L1–L3 under the confirmed meter. Never hand-typed, for the reason in §1.

§4

Why structure and matra must be separate layers

The same syllable is worth different amounts in different meters. This is the single most important thing for an annotator to understand, because it explains why they are not asked to count matra at all.

শব্দ
শব্closed দopen
অক্ষরবৃত্ত — closed syllable counts 2 only at a word end; শব্ is word-medial → 1 + 1 = 2 matra
মাত্রাবৃত্ত — closed syllable counts 2 everywhere → 2 + 1 = 3 matra
স্বরবৃত্ত — every syllable counts 1, open or closed → 1 + 1 = 2 matra

One structural annotation, three different matra readings, all produced by rule. If the annotator had written "3", the corpus would silently encode a meter assumption.

A line of entirely open syllables — চলে বাঁকে বাঁকে, six open syllables — reads as 6 matra under all three, which is why such lines are useless for training and should not dominate the pilot sample.

The discriminating rule is the closed-syllable column, so a pilot sample should be selected for lines that are rich in closed syllables. Sampling at random will over-represent lines that cannot distinguish the meters.

To verify Three things: whether "word end" in akkharbritta means the orthographic word or the phonological word; whether matrabritta admits a 2-matra open syllable in any position; and whether the svarabritta foot is better described by stress than by count, which would change what the annotator marks in L3.
§6

Hard cases

These decide agreement. Each needs a ruling from the pinned authority; the rulings below are the draft's best guess and are flagged accordingly.

1. Inherent vowel deletionঅ-লোপ

Written কমল has three orthographic aksharas but is pronounced with two syllables, the second closed. Bangla orthography systematically over-counts syllables this way.

Draft rule — যাচাই Syllabify by pronunciation, not orthography. Meter follows the spoken line; an orthographic syllabifier will mis-scan most of the corpus.
2. Conjunct consonantsযুক্তাক্ষর

A word-medial conjunct usually closes the preceding syllable. A word-initial conjunct does not close anything, since nothing precedes it.

Draft rule — যাচাই Split the conjunct across the syllable boundary when medial; keep it with the following vowel when initial. Record the conjunct's identity — this is the feature a subword tokeniser destroys, and the reason to use a byte-level model.
3. Ref and phalaরেফ ও ফলা

A রেফ closes the syllable it sits on. র-ফলা and য-ফলা generally do not add a syllable, but য-ফলা often signals gemination, which does close the preceding syllable.

Draft rule — যাচাই Ref closes. R-phala does not. Y-phala closes the preceding syllable when it geminates — an annotator judgement, so flag it for adjudication rather than guessing.
4. Sadhu and chalit in one poemসাধু / চলিত

Nineteenth-century verse is sadhu; much twentieth-century verse is chalit; some poems mix registers deliberately for effect.

Draft rule Tag register at L0 per line, not per poem. A mixed poem is data, not a defect.
5. Anusvara, chandrabindu, visargaং ঁ ঃ

These behave differently: one closes a syllable, one is nasalisation on the vowel, one is rare in modern verse.

Draft rule — যাচাই ং closes the syllable. ঁ does not add or close. ঃ is decided case by case and flagged.
6. Diphthongsঐ ঔ

One syllable or two, and the answer may differ between the written vowel and the same sequence spelled out.

Draft rule — যাচাই Treat as one syllable by default; flag any line where that makes the foot count fail, since those lines are the evidence for the opposite rule.
7. Foreign and Sanskritic loansতৎসম / বিদেশি

Tatsama words often keep the inherent vowel that a native word would drop; English loans in modern verse follow neither pattern.

Draft rule Annotate as pronounced in the poem's own register, and tag the token as a loan so the disagreement is measurable rather than invisible.
8. Metrical licenceছন্দের স্বাধীনতা

Poets stretch or compress to fit; a good poet's deviation is deliberate.

Draft rule Annotate what the text says, then flag licence=true where the line only scans under a stretched reading. Never silently re-syllabify to make a line fit the expected count — that is the single most damaging thing an annotator can do here.
9. Foot boundaries inside a wordপর্ব-সীমা

A foot boundary can fall mid-word, which annotators resist because it looks wrong.

Draft rule — যাচাই Permitted. Expect this to be the lowest-agreement layer and budget adjudication time for it.
10. Free verseগদ্যছন্দ

Much of the modern canon does not scan under any of the three meters.

Draft rule Label meter=none and still annotate L1–L2. Excluding free verse entirely would make a model that has never seen a negative example.
§7

Out of scope

  • Rhyme. Separate annotation task with a separate guideline; it needs phonology this one does not.
  • Performance timing. Recited duration is not textual meter; sung Rabindra Sangeet is a different object again.
  • Quality or aesthetic judgement. Never. Nothing in this corpus says whether a poem is good.
  • Poet attribution and dating. Carried in poem-level provenance, never inferred by the annotator.
§8

Annotation format

One JSON object per line of verse. Syllables carry character offsets into the original so nothing depends on the annotation tool's tokenisation.

{
  "poem_id": "tagore-sonar-tari-014",
  "line_no": 3,
  "text": "…",                    // the line exactly as printed
  "register": "chalit",          // sadhu | chalit | mixed
  "syllables": [
    {"i": 0, "span": [0, 3], "type": "closed", "word_final": false,
     "conjunct": true, "flags": []},
    {"i": 1, "span": [3, 4], "type": "open",   "word_final": true,
     "conjunct": false, "flags": ["loan"]}
  ],
  "feet": [[0, 3], [4, 7]],      // half-open syllable index ranges
  "short_final_foot": true,      // অতিপর্ব present
  "meter": {"proposed": "akkharbritta",   // from the rule engine
            "confirmed": "akkharbritta",  // annotator's call
            "override_reason": null},
  "licence": false,
  "annotator": "A2",
  "notes": ""
}

Matra is deliberately absent. It is computed from this object plus the meter rules, and the computation is the thing under test.

§9

Agreement protocol

Three annotators, 300 lines, all three annotating the first 100 independently and the remaining 200 split with a 20 percent overlap.

LayerMeasureExpectation
L1 syllabificationboundary F1High. A low score means the guideline failed, not the annotators.
L2 open / closedCohen's κ per pairHigh, and the one layer where a rule engine should nearly match a human.
L3 foot boundariesboundary F1Lowest of the four. Expect it and report it.
L4 meter classFleiss' κHigh on metrical verse; the interesting disagreement is free verse versus loose matrabritta.

Do not set a target number in advance and quietly revise the guideline until it is met. Report the pilot's figures, state which hard case from §6 drove each disagreement, then revise once and re-run. The revision history is a contribution in itself — it is the part other low-resource prosody projects can reuse.

Adjudication. A fourth pass by the prosody specialist resolves flagged lines. Adjudicated lines stay marked, so a model trained on them can be evaluated with and without.

§10

What to settle before annotation starts

  1. The authority and edition (§2), and whether পয়ার is a label in its own right.
  2. The closed-syllable column of the matra table (§5) — the rest of the design rests on it.
  3. Rulings on hard cases 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 9, which are the ones the draft marks যাচাই.
  4. Whether the corpus is poem-sampled or line-sampled. Line sampling gives better meter coverage; poem sampling lets you evaluate whole-poem consistency. The metric paper wants whole poems.
  5. Provenance rules for the source texts — edition, and public-domain status per poem, recorded per poem rather than assumed for a poet.
